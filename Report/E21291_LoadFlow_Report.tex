\section{Theoretical Formulation}
The power flow analysis is based on the nodal power balance equations derived from Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL). For a system with $N$ buses, the complex power injection $S_i$ at bus $i$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
S_i = P_i + jQ_i = V_i I_i^* = V_i \left( \sum_{k=1}^{N} Y_{ik} V_k \right)^*
\end{equation}
Expressing voltages in polar form $V_i = |V_i| \angle \delta_i$ and admittance as $Y_{ik} = |Y_{ik}| \angle \theta_{ik}$:
\begin{equation}
P_i = \sum_{k=1}^{N} |V_i| |V_k| |Y_{ik}| \cos(\theta_{ik} - \delta_i + \delta_k)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Q_i = -\sum_{k=1}^{N} |V_i| |V_k| |Y_{ik}| \sin(\theta_{ik} - \delta_i + \delta_k)
\end{equation}
The Newton-Raphson method solves the mismatch equations $[\Delta P, \Delta Q]^T = -J [\Delta \delta, \Delta |V|/|V|]^T$. The Jacobian elements are derived as:
\begin{align}
J1_{ik} &= \frac{\partial P_i}{\partial \delta_k} = -|V_i| |V_k| |Y_{ik}| \sin(\theta_{ik} - \delta_i + \delta_k) \quad (i \neq k) \\
J2_{ik} &= \frac{\partial P_i}{\partial |V_k|} |V_k| = |V_i| |V_k| |Y_{ik}| \cos(\theta_{ik} - \delta_i + \delta_k) \quad (i \neq k)
\end{align}
These analytical derivatives ensure quadratic convergence.

\section{Task 1: Program Development}

\subsection{Methodology}
A customized Newton-Raphson load flow solver was developed in Python. The program was designed to solve the power flow equations for any general power system, with specific application to the IEEE 9-bus system.

\subsubsection{Key Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Y-Bus Construction:} The admittance matrix $Y_{bus}$ is built automatically from line and transformer data. Off-nominal tap ratios are handled by modifying the $\pi$-equivalent model of the transformers.
    \item \textbf{Jacobian Formulation:} The Jacobian matrix is composed of four sub-matrices representing the partial derivatives of Real (P) and Reactive (Q) power with respect to Voltage Angle ($\delta$) and Magnitude ($|V|$):
    \[
    J = \begin{bmatrix}
    \frac{\partial P}{\partial \delta} & \frac{\partial P}{\partial |V|} \\
    \frac{\partial Q}{\partial \delta} & \frac{\partial Q}{\partial |V|}
    \end{bmatrix}
    \]
    \item \textbf{Bus Type Handling:} The program dynamically identifies:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Slack Bus (Type 3):} Bus 1 (Voltage magnitude and angle fixed).
        \item \textbf{PV Buses (Type 2):} Buses 2, 3 (Active power and Voltage magnitude fixed).
        \item \textbf{PQ Buses (Type 1):} Buses 4-9 (Active and Reactive power fixed).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mismatch Equations:} The iterative process minimizes the mismatches:
    \[ \Delta P_i = P_{spec,i} - P_{calc,i} \]
    \[ \Delta Q_i = Q_{spec,i} - Q_{calc,i} \]
    A strict tolerance of $1 \times 10^{-4}$ pu (approx. 10 kW/kVar) is used.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Algorithm Implementation}
The implementation follows the standard Newton-Raphson algorithm structural flow:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Initialization:} Data input and Flat Start ($|V|=1.0$ pu, $\delta=0^\circ$).
    \item \textbf{Admittance Matrix:} Construction of the complex $Y_{bus}$ matrix.
    \item \textbf{Iteration Loop:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Calculate Power Mismatches ($\Delta P, \Delta Q$).
        \item Check Convergence ($max(|\Delta P|, |\Delta Q|) < \epsilon$).
        \item Construct Jacobian Matrix ($J$).
        \item Solve Linear System ($J \cdot [\Delta \delta, \Delta |V|]^T = [\Delta P, \Delta Q]^T$).
        \item Update State Variables ($|V|, \delta$).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Post-Processing:} Calculation of line flows and losses.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Engineering Discussion and Analysis}

\subsubsection{Theoretical Basis of Q-V Coupling}
The simulation results clearly demonstrate a fundamental principle of power systems: \textbf{Voltage magnitudes are strongly coupled with Reactive Power (Q) flows}, while Voltage Angles are coupled with Active Power (P) flows.
From the simplified short line approximation, the voltage drop $\Delta V$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
\Delta V \approx \frac{R P + X Q}{V}
\end{equation}
In transmission systems, the X/R ratio is typically high ($X \gg R$). For the IEEE 9-bus system lines (e.g., Line 4-5), $X \approx 0.085$ while $R \approx 0.01$. Substituting this inequality:
\begin{equation}
\Delta V \approx \frac{X Q}{V}
\end{equation}
This explains why varying $Q$ by 10\% causes a much larger deviation in voltage (spread between red lines in Fig. \ref{fig:sens_profiles}) compared to varying $P$ (green lines). The calculation shows Bus 5 voltage swinging from 0.9896 pu (+10\% Q) to 1.0016 pu (-10\% Q), a range of \textbf{0.012 pu}. In contrast, P variation only changes it by \textbf{0.0014 pu}.

\subsubsection{Topological Vulnerability Assessment}
\textbf{Bus 5} is consistently identified as the weakest point in the network.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Electrical Distance:} Bus 5 is electrically distant from the generation sources (PV Buses 2, 3 and Slack Bus 1). The impedance path to these sources is high, increasing the voltage drop.
    \item \textbf{Load Concentration:} It serves a substantial load (125 MW, 50 MVAr).
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity:} The standard deviation of voltages at Bus 5 is the highest in the system. This indicates that Bus 5 operates closer to the "nose" of the P-V/Q-V curve than other buses.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Implications for System Operation}
The comparison between P and Q effects suggests that traditional demand side management (shifting Active Power load) is ineffective for voltage control. Instead, \textbf{Reactive Power Compensation} is the required solution.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Shunt Capacitors:} Installing switchable capacitor banks at Bus 5 would inject local reactive power ($Q_c$), effectively reducing the $Q$ flow requirement from the lines, thus reducing the $XQ$ voltage drop.
    \item \textbf{Voltage Collapse Limit:} Since Bus 5 shows high sensitivity ($dV/dQ$), the system margin to voltage collapse is smallest here. Operators should monitor Bus 5 voltage as the critical system indicator.
\end{enumerate}
